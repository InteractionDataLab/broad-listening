Case study 2 · Médecins Sans Frontières public reports

What Médecins Sans Frontières writes about, country by country

Each dot is a passage from a public report. Passages that say similar things sit close together, and each colour is a theme.

893 stories · 3,912 passages · 8 countries · 2023–2025 · 9 themes

We collected 1,481 public reports of Médecins Sans Frontières on eight countries, 2023 to 2025, from ReliefWeb. 1,362 had usable text. Many stories exist in two or three languages, so we kept each story once: 893 stories, cut into 3,912 passages. We grouped the passages by meaning and asked which country is reported for what, and whether it changed over time. Md Nuruzzaman and Liubov Tupikina · Interaction Data Lab, INSERM U1338 · November 2026

Before quoting a number: a share shows how often the reports talk about a topic. It does not measure how common a problem is in a country. Themes were found and named by machine, and no person has coded a sample yet. Half of the passages were joined to the nearest sub-theme afterwards. How much to trust this →

Themes

What do the reports talk about?

Two themes lead the reports. “Wounded patients and injuries” comes up in 58% of stories, and “displacement and access restrictions” in 45%. Every other theme is in fewer than a quarter of the stories.

Each bar is the share of the 893 stories that touch the theme (at least one passage in it). The black line is the 95% range: the true value is very likely inside it. The ◇ shows the share of the 3,912 passages instead. We count stories so that each report has one vote. Click a bar to see the theme on the map.2

Did the themes change over the three years?

Little changed over the three years. Earthquake stories fell from 16% in 2023 to 3% in 2024 and 6% in 2025. Among sub-themes, “aid restriction” rose every year, from 16% to 23% to 30%.

In percentage points per year: earthquake −4.3 (95% range −7.0 to −1.7), aid restriction +7.0 (3.6 to 10.5). The earthquake theme mostly comes from Syria (45% of Syria’s stories) and Myanmar (33% of Myanmar’s).

“Psychological trauma symptoms” also differs between years (8%, 13%, 6%), as a bump in 2024, with no steady trend.3

Country

Which country is reported for what?

Every theme differs between countries. Yemen stories are about outbreaks (52%) and maternity care (43%), Syria about the earthquake (45%), Ukraine about wounded patients (70%) and trauma (49%), Palestine about displacement and access (65%).

This shows what the reports choose to tell. Each story counts once, under the country it was collected for. The result stays the same with stories about one country only, except for “migrant routes and asylum access”.3

A country with fewer than 30 stories is marked “few”: its shares are less precise.

Medical topics

What does a fixed health checklist find?

Each country has its own health profile. DR Congo stories often mention infectious disease (67%), Sudan stories malnutrition (64%), Ukraine stories mental health (60%), and Yemen stories maternal and newborn care (59%).

Here a computer did not group anything: each topic is a fixed list of words, and a story counts when its text uses them. Only stories about one country are counted. When a language model checked samples, 19 or 20 of every 20 matches were right. We did not measure how many mentions the lists miss, so read each share as “at least about this much”. For 8 of the 12 topics the check could not clearly tell matches from non-matches.4

Limits

How much can you trust this?

This is a first map of what the reports say, and no person has checked it yet.

Two checks pass. Redoing the grouping on 80% of the passages gave nearly the same themes (agreement 0.86 on a 0 to 1 scale, adjusted Rand; our minimum was 0.75). A language model, used as a second reader on 180 passages, agreed with the themes at 0.72 (Cohen’s kappa; minimum 0.60).

Two checks warn. On a scale from “no themes at all” (0) to “clean, separate themes” (1), these passages sit at 0.42, below our minimum of 0.50: the themes overlap. A second text model, run on the same passages, fully found none of the 15 sub-themes and partly found 6.

Small gaps can be missed. Comparing one country with the rest, the data can reliably show a gap of about 5 percentage points for Palestine, but only of 29 points or more for Yemen (80% power).

Machine reading, no person yetThe grouping, the theme names and the second reader are machines. No person has coded a sample of passages by hand to compare.
Visibility in the reportsA share says how often the reports mention a topic. Countries in the news get more reports, whatever the size of the problem.
Half the passages were joined laterThe grouping left 2,027 of 3,912 passages (52%) unplaced; each was then joined to the nearest sub-theme. On the map, one click hides them.
One theme is mixed“Displacement and access restrictions” joins sub-themes that do not fit well together; its report on the map says so. 188 stories also carry other country tags; each still counts once.
Do the themes and the word-list topics agree?

The two readings agree where they should. 84% of the stories in “sexual violence survivors” mention sexual violence care, 4.8 times the usual rate. The same holds for maternity and maternal care (86%), trauma and mental health (86%), and outbreaks and infectious disease (95%).

The themes were found by meaning, without any word list; the topics come from fixed word lists. Two different methods pointing the same way support each other. Big themes touch many topics, because long stories mention many things.4
