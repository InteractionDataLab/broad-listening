About this project

A pipeline for finding themes in open-ended texts, and two worked examples of what it produces.

Who

This work was supervised by Marc Santolini (Interaction Data Lab, INSERM U1338) and co-supervised by Dr Liubov Tupikina (Learning Planet Institute). They guided it at every step. It began during a research internship of Md Nuruzzaman (M1 AIRE, Université Paris Cité, within the ANR project Open Futures) and continued in a second internship at the Interaction Data Lab. The public-reports example comes from a workshop project at the Learning Planet Institute, supervised by Dr Liubov Tupikina.

The starting point was more than 1,500 open answers from the iGEM 2025 survey. Those answers were the richest part of the survey, and also the part nobody had time to read. So we looked for a way to read thousands of answers without losing what each person says. On this site we show what the method finds, and also where it is still weak, so that you can judge it for yourself.

What the pipeline does

It cleans a set of short texts and turns each into a vector of numbers with a multilingual embedding model. It then groups similar texts, names each group with a language model, and tests how themes differ between groups of people. Every step is allowed to fail, and a theme that fails a check is marked as such.

Data and what we show

Survey answers. Participants of the iGEM 2025 Jamboree answered a survey with five open questions. The answers are shown with team names and people’s names replaced by tags such as [name] or [city], from the analysis run of 2 November 2026. No team name, participant identifier or country is stored with an answer, the answers are stored in a shuffled order, and no answer is shown with its country, team or award. Groups such as countries or award levels appear only as counts and shares of people. Showing the answers this way was agreed with Marc Santolini in November 2026.

Public reports. Public reports of Médecins Sans Frontières, collected from ReliefWeb on 16 September 2026. Passages are shown as published, each with a link to its source. This site is not made by, and is not linked to, Médecins Sans Frontières.
