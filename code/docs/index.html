broadlisten — theme explorers

1,505 open-ended survey responses from a challenge-based learning programme, grouped into 39 themes. These are the figures from the analysis, with the responses themselves withheld.

The map

  • Themes in two dimensions All 1,505 responses, projected with seeded UMAP at a faithfulness of 0.895. Hover a point, click a theme in the legend to isolate it, zoom. Grey points are the responses the clustering left unplaced.
  • Themes in three dimensions The same corpus at a faithfulness of 0.920. Drag to rotate.

What the study found

  • Sentiment across the corpus 1,475 responses received a verdict; 30 were refused because the model could not quote the span it claimed to describe. The refusals are on the chart, because leaving them off would raise every other bar.
  • Theme prevalence How much of the corpus each of the 39 themes accounts for, with 95% Wilson intervals, and the unplaced share shown alongside.
  • What each theme may be used for 18 Claimable, 17 Descriptive, 4 Artifact. A downgraded theme keeps its place in every descriptive figure; it loses the right to carry a finding, not its right to exist.

No participant wrote anything you can read on this site. Every figure here was built with include_text=False, so the responses behind the points are not in the files. Each one was then checked by reading the written file back and searching it for verbatim responses, for all 125 team names, and for the participant identifiers. The build refuses to finish if it finds any.

Four themes read [label withheld]. Those are the ones whose labels failed verification, and the package does not print a label its own checks rejected.

Every chart on this page is counts and proportions. Nothing a participant wrote appears in any of them.

Two figures from the original analysis are deliberately absent: the team profile and team award explorers. Both are keyed by team, and withholding response text does nothing about that — a named team of a few people, with a country and a topic, is identifiable regardless.

Built with broadlisten, an Apache-2.0 package for finding themes in open-ended survey text. By Md Nuruzzaman, Liubov Tupikina and Marc Santolini, during a Master's research internship (M1 AIRE, Université Paris Cité), within the ANR project Open Futures.
